\section{Overall findings}
\label{sec:results:overall}

Across all experiments, we find that:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Neighborhood size matters, but mainly at the first step:} moving from \(NB=1\) to \(NB\ge2\) yields a large improvement in stability and quality.
\item \textbf{Bigger is not always better:} for \(NB\ge2\), performance does not improve monotonically and depends on the metric and rollout horizon.
\item \textbf{Evaluation protocol is critical:} differences across neighborhood sizes are easiest to interpret when training and evaluation are consistent (sampling/temperature choices) and when models are assessed at multiple rollout horizons.
\end{itemize}

\section{Thesis takeaway}
\label{sec:results:takeaway}

The main takeaway of this thesis is that MNCA can model tissue-like dynamics in a controlled synthetic setting, but careful experimental design is required to obtain interpretable conclusions.
In particular, the neighborhood-size effect can be masked by training/evaluation confounds when models already fail at long-horizon rollouts.
